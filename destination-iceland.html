Iceland

Seven days on the Ring Road, or four on the south coast. Here is what it costs, when to go, and the parts that go wrong.

Copy the 7-day itinerary Build your own
N 64.1466 / W 21.9426 · SEC 01 · OVERVIEW

Overview

Iceland is the easiest hard trip an American can take. Five to seven hours from the East Coast, everyone speaks English, the tap water is the best you will ever drink, and the country will still fine you $200 for parking in the wrong gravel lot.

The island is roughly the size of Kentucky with the population of Bakersfield. Almost everything a first-time visitor wants to see sits within twenty miles of Route 1, the Ring Road, which loops 828 miles around the coast. You do not need a guide, a tour bus, or Icelandic. You do need a car, a rain shell, and a realistic view of how far you can drive in a day when the speed limit is 55 mph and there is a waterfall every eleven minutes.

The two honest itineraries are: four days on the south coast, out and back from Reykjavík, or seven to ten days on the full Ring Road going counterclockwise. Anything shorter than four days is a Golden Circle day trip with an expensive flight attached. Anything that tries to do the Ring Road in five days is 250 miles of driving a day and you will remember the car, not the country.

The other thing nobody says out loud: Iceland is expensive in a way that surprises people who have been to Norway. A gas station hot dog is $6. A sit-down dinner for two with one beer each runs $110. Grocery stores, specifically Bónus and Krónan, are how residents live and how you should eat at least half your meals.

Flight time from the US
5h 20m from Boston, 5h 50m from Newark, 7h 10m from Seattle, 8h 05m from Chicago via a connection
Currency
Icelandic króna. Cards work everywhere including gas pumps and public bathrooms. Carry no cash.
Language
Icelandic. English is near universal in tourism and service.
Time zone
GMT year round. Four hours ahead of Eastern, seven ahead of Pacific.
Passport
Required, valid at least three months beyond your departure date. No visa for US citizens for stays under 90 days.
Plug
Type F, 230V. You need an adapter, and a converter for anything with a heating element.

Three ways to cut $600 without suffering

  • Buy groceries at Bónus in Reykjavík on day one and pack lunches. Saves roughly $22 per person per day.
  • Book a guesthouse with a shared bathroom instead of a hotel. Saves $60 to $90 a night, and the showers are geothermal either way.
  • Skip the Blue Lagoon for a municipal pool. Every town has one, entry is $8 to $12, and locals are in it.

Three day sample plan: the south coast

If you only have four days on the ground, this is the plan. Out from Reykjavík along Route 1, as far east as your appetite for driving allows, then back.

  • 6:40 AM Land at Keflavík (KEF)

    Most US red-eyes land between 6:00 and 7:30 AM. Passport control is fast before 7:00 and brutal after 8:00 when three widebodies arrive together.

    Transit Travel 50 min · 31 mi to city $0
  • 8:30 AM Pick up the rental car

    Take the gravel protection and the sand-and-ash waiver. Both are cheap and both are the two things Iceland actually damages.

    Transit Travel 20 min shuttle $92 per day, 4x4
  • 10:15 AM Sky Lagoon or Blue Lagoon soak

    A hot soak is a better jet-lag fix than a nap you will regret. Book the earliest slot; both sell out weeks ahead in summer.

    Activity Travel 35 min $69 per person
  • 2:00 PM Reykjavík on foot

    Hallgrímskirkja tower, then downhill along Skólavörðustígur to the harbour. Two hours covers the whole downtown.

    Sightseeing Travel 15 min $9 tower
  • 7:00 PM Dinner in the Grandi district

    Fish stew, lamb soup and rye bread. Reserve two days out from June through August or you will eat at 9:30 PM.

    Food Travel 10 min $48 per person
Estimated daily cost $218 per person

Two travelers, one rental 4x4 split between them, one night in a Reykjavík guesthouse at $118 per person.

    Sightseeing · 45m Travel 50 min · 37 mi $0

    Use the upper viewpoint when it is windy. The lower path ices from October and the railing does not inspire confidence.

    Sightseeing · 1h Travel 12 min · 6 mi $0
  • 4:30 PM Seljalandsfoss and Gljufrabui

    You can walk behind Seljalandsfoss and you will get soaked doing it. Gljufrabui is a five minute walk north and half the crowd.

    Sightseeing · 1h Travel 1h 35m · 76 mi $8 parking
  • 7:30 PM Check in at Vík

    Fill the tank in Hvolsvöllur, not in Vík. The price difference is real and the Vík pump is often the only one open late.

    Lodging Travel 45 min · 33 mi $93 per person

N 64.9631 / W 19.0208 · SEC 05 · LOGISTICS

Getting around

Rent a car. There is no realistic alternative unless you plan to stay in Reykjavík and take day tours, which costs more and shows you less. Public buses between towns exist but run once or twice a day.

A 2WD compact is fine from June through September if you stay on Route 1 and the paved side roads. You need a 4x4 for F-roads in the highlands, and F-roads are legally closed to 2WD vehicles regardless of how the weather looks.

Three rules that cost people money every summer:

  • Never open a car door into the wind. Wind-torn doors are the single most common rental damage claim in Iceland and are almost never covered.
  • Check road.is and vedur.is every morning. Road closures and wind warnings are posted there first and they are authoritative.
  • Off-road driving is a criminal offense. Fines start around $2,800. The moss takes decades to regrow, which is why they take it seriously.

From Keflavík to Reykjavík without a car, the Flybus runs on every arrival and costs about $35 each way.

N 64.9631 / W 19.0208 · SEC 06 · LODGING

Where to stay

Book lodging before you book anything else between June and August. Iceland has fewer hotel rooms than Cleveland, and the south coast towns sell out four months ahead.

Reykjavík

Stay downtown within the 101 postcode and you can walk to everything. Expect $180 to $290 a night for a mid-range double. Guesthouses on Laugavegur are noisier and $70 cheaper.

1 to 2 nights$118 pp

Vík and Hvolsvöllur

The two practical bases for the south coast. Hvolsvöllur is 40 minutes closer to Reykjavík and roughly $40 cheaper. Vík puts you next to the black sand beach at sunrise.

2 nights$93 pp

Höfn and the east

Only worth it on a Ring Road trip. Rooms are limited, the drive is long, and the payoff is langoustine and near-empty roads for two days.

1 to 2 nights$104 pp

Camper vans, honestly

A camper van looks like it saves money and often does not. A two-berth van runs $180 to $240 a day in summer, which is more than a car plus a guesthouse. Wild camping outside designated sites has been illegal since 2015. Vans make sense if you value schedule freedom more than a shower, and not really otherwise.

N 64.9631 / W 19.0208 · SEC 07 · SAFETY

Safety notes

Iceland has one of the lowest violent crime rates on earth. Everything that hurts visitors here is weather, water or terrain.

  • Reynisfjara sneaker waves. The most dangerous tourist site in the country. Waves arrive far above the visible waterline with no warning and the undertow is fatal. Stay uphill of the wet sand line, always, no exceptions for a photograph.
  • Wind. Gusts over 45 mph are routine and over 90 mph happen several times a winter. When vedur.is posts an orange warning, that is the day you stay put.
  • River crossings. Do not drive through water in a rental. It voids every insurance product sold in Iceland, without exception.
  • Glaciers and ice caves. Never walk on a glacier without a guide and crampons. Crevasses are covered by snow and are not visible.
  • Geothermal areas. The ground crust near hot springs is thin. Stay on marked boardwalks. Burns from stepping through are severe and evacuation is slow.

Numbers to save before you fly

  • Emergency services: 112
  • SafeTravel alerts: text 112 Iceland app, free
  • Road conditions: road.is
  • Weather and warnings: vedur.is
  • US Embassy Reykjavík: +354 595 2200

Enroll in the State Department STEP program before any international trip. It is free and it is how the embassy reaches you if something happens.

Travel medical coverage matters more here than in Europe. Search and rescue in Iceland is volunteer-run and free, but a hospital transfer or an early flight home is not. Read our disclaimer before relying on any figure on this page.
