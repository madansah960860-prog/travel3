Reading the totals

Time on the move adds up every travel-time value across the whole trip. If a single day is over five hours, you are driving a vacation, not taking one.

Trip total multiplies the per-person cost by your traveler count. It deliberately excludes airfare, because airfare moves daily and pretending otherwise makes the number useless.

Six rules that make a day plan survive

1. Three anchors a day, maximum

One big thing in the morning, one in the afternoon, one meal worth planning. Anything past that is a wish list. The fourth stop is the one you skip in a bad mood at 4:00 PM.

2. Price the day, not the trip

Trip totals hide overspending. A daily number tells you on day two that you are $40 a day over, while you can still do something about it.

3. Put the travel time in, honestly

Mapping apps give you the drive. They do not give you parking, the walk from the lot, the bathroom line, or the twenty minutes you lose finding the trailhead. Add 20 percent.

4. Leave one empty block per three days

Weather, a closed road, a late train, or a place you liked enough to stay in. A plan with no slack is a plan that breaks on day two and never recovers.

5. Check sunset, not just opening hours

Reykjavík in November gets about five hours of usable light. Anchorage in June gets nineteen. The same itinerary is a different trip depending on the month.

6. Write the note now

Reservation windows, permit numbers, the fact that the ferry only runs Tuesday. You will not remember it in four months, and the note prints on the day card.

N 45.5152 / W 122.6784 · SEC 03 · LIMITS

What this page can and cannot do

This planner is the real free tier. It saves to your browser, it has no account, and it does not phone home. If you clear your browser data, the plan goes with it.

It can: build up to fourteen days, sort stops by time automatically, total cost and travel time, print to PDF through your browser, and reload where you left off.

It cannot: sync between devices, share with a collaborator, alert you on price drops, or store more than one trip at a time. Those live in a Hardpack account.
