Accessibility Statement

What we target, what we have done, what we know is imperfect, and how to tell us when something does not work.

Effective date: 06/01/2026 Last updated: 06/01/2026 Hardpack Trip Co. Portland, Oregon, USA

Please note: this is a starting template written for our business. Have a licensed attorney review and adapt it before relying on it for yours. Questions about this page go to hello@hardpack.travel.

N 45.5152 / W 122.6784 · SEC 01 · ACCESS

Full text

1. Our commitment

Hardpack Trip Co. is committed to making hardpack.travel usable by as many people as possible, including people who use screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, screen magnification, voice control, or who need reduced motion.

We target conformance with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA. This statement describes where we currently stand against that target.

2. What we have implemented

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmarks: a header, a primary navigation region, a main region and a footer, with one h1 and a logical heading order beneath it.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable element on every page is a skip link that jumps past the navigation.
  • Keyboard operation. Every control, including the mobile menu, the FAQ accordions, the day tabs in the trip planner, the cookie banner and the destination filters, is reachable and operable with a keyboard alone. Tab order follows the visual order.
  • Visible focus. Focus is indicated by a 3 pixel safety orange outline with a 2 pixel offset. We never remove focus styling.
  • Contrast. Body text is at least 4.5:1 against its background and large text at least 3:1. The safety orange in our palette is deliberately never used as text on the concrete background, because that pairing measures 2.65:1 and fails. Orange is used as a fill, a rule or as text on black only.
  • Images. Every content image has descriptive alternative text. Purely decorative elements, including the baseline grid overlay and the cursor crosshair, are hidden from assistive technology.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label above it, help text where useful, and an error message directly below the field it belongs to. Errors are announced, and invalid fields are marked with aria-invalid rather than colour alone.
  • Reduced motion. When your system requests reduced motion, the marquee stops, snap scrolling is disabled, the cursor crosshair is switched off entirely and all transitions collapse. Nothing on this site depends on motion to be understood.
  • Zoom and reflow. Content reflows to a single column and remains usable at 400 percent zoom and at a 320 pixel viewport width. Nothing scrolls horizontally except tables, which scroll inside their own container.
  • No motion or audio traps. No autoplaying audio, no autoplaying video, no content that flashes more than three times per second, no popups that block content on load.
  • Grid overlay control. The decorative baseline grid can be switched off with the Grid button in the sidebar, and the choice is remembered.

3. Known limitations

We would rather list these than pretend they do not exist.

  • The trip planner is a data entry interface with several related controls. It is fully keyboard operable, but it is denser than the rest of the site and takes more tab presses to move through.
  • Wide comparison tables scroll horizontally inside their container on small screens. The scroll region is keyboard focusable, but reading a seven-column table on a 360 pixel screen is inherently awkward.
  • If we introduce third-party advertising, we will control its placement and its label but not the accessibility of the creative inside it. No ad units run on the site today.
  • Some third-party partner sites we link to do not meet WCAG 2.1 AA. We have no control over them.

4. How we test

Manual keyboard testing on every template, screen reader spot checks with VoiceOver on macOS and NVDA on Windows, automated checks with an accessibility linter in our build process, and contrast ratios calculated for every colour pair in our design system before it ships. We re-test the whole site after any change to the shared stylesheet.

5. Compatibility

This site is designed to work with current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop and mobile, together with the assistive technology built into those platforms. JavaScript is required for the trip planner, the checklists and the cookie banner. All editorial content, navigation and forms are readable and usable without it.

6. Feedback, and what happens next

If any part of this site is not usable for you, tell us and we will fix it. Please include the page address, what you were trying to do, and the browser and assistive technology you were using.

This statement was prepared on 06/01/2026 based on our own evaluation of the site against WCAG 2.1 Level AA. It has not been reviewed by an independent third-party auditor. We update it whenever the site changes materially.
