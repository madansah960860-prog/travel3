National parks

Ten to fourteen days, one rental car, and the reservation systems nobody warns you about until the gate turns you around.

Copy the Utah 5-day itinerary Read the permits guide
N 37.2982 / W 113.0263 · SEC 01 · OVERVIEW

Overview

The American national park road trip is the trip most people underestimate the hardest. Not because it is difficult, but because the map lies about distance and the reservation system has changed a lot since 2020.

There are 63 national parks and 433 units in the system. Nobody sees them in one trip. The practical decision is which of four regions you are doing, because each is a self-contained loop with a different airport, a different season and a different set of rules.

The Utah Mighty 5. Zion, Bryce, Capitol Reef, Arches, Canyonlands. Fly into Las Vegas or Salt Lake City. Five to eight days. The best density of scenery per mile in the country and the cheapest of the four loops.

California Sierra. Yosemite, Sequoia, Kings Canyon, plus Death Valley in winter. Fly into Fresno, Sacramento or San Francisco. Yosemite has the strictest reservation system in the park service and the highest lodging costs.

Greater Yellowstone. Yellowstone and Grand Teton. Fly into Jackson Hole or Bozeman. Six to nine days. Lodging inside both parks books thirteen months out.

Pacific Northwest. Olympic, Mount Rainier, North Cascades. Fly into Seattle. Three parks within four hours of one airport, which makes it the easiest logistics of the four.

What kills trips here is drive time. Zion to Arches is 5 hours. Yellowstone's own Grand Loop is 142 miles of 45 mph road with bison-caused stops. Plan 250 miles as a hard daily ceiling and you will actually enjoy it.

Where the money actually goes wrong

  • Gateway town lodging in peak season. Springdale and Moab hit $290 a night in April and October. Staying 25 minutes out cuts that by a third.
  • One-way rental drop fees. Las Vegas in, Salt Lake City out can add $180 to $340. Loop back to the same airport unless the time saved is worth it.
  • Food inside the parks. A sandwich at a park lodge is $16. Groceries in the gateway town are normal price. Pack lunches every day.

The first three days of the Utah loop, from the airport to the Moab base. It ends where the Arches and Canyonlands days begin.

01 Las Vegas → Springdale, Zion Southwest loop · Day 1 of 3
  • 7:30 AM Pick up the rental at LAS

    Book a mid-size SUV, not a compact. You will be carrying four gallons of water, a cooler and gear, and the clearance helps on unpaved pullouts.

    Transit Travel 25 min shuttle $62 per day
  • 9:00 AM Grocery and water stop, North Las Vegas

    Last real supermarket for 160 miles. One gallon of water per person per day, plus a bag of ice and a cheap foam cooler.

    Supply Travel 20 min · 12 mi $34 per person
  • 12:45 PM Zion entrance and visitor center

    Buy the America the Beautiful annual pass here for $80 if you are visiting three or more parks. It pays for itself at park three.

    Park entry Travel 2h 40m · 162 mi $80 annual pass
  • 2:00 PM Shuttle to the Riverside Walk

    The canyon road is shuttle-only from March through November. Riverside Walk is 2.2 miles round trip, flat, paved, and the gateway to the Narrows.

    Hike · 2h Travel 40 min shuttle $0
  • 6:30 PM Check in, Springdale

    Springdale sits at the park gate and is walkable to the shuttle stop. Rooms run $190 to $310 in season and sell out in April.

    Lodging Travel 15 min $112 per person
Estimated daily cost $188 per person

Two travelers splitting one vehicle. The annual pass is a one-time cost counted on day one and used for the whole trip.

02 Zion → Bryce Canyon Southwest loop · Day 2 of 3
  • 5:45 AM Angels Landing, permit required

    The chain section requires a permit from a seasonal or day-before lottery. Without one, hike to Scout Lookout instead, which is the same view minus the last half mile.

    Hike · 4h Travel 30 min shuttle $6 lottery fee
  • 11:00 AM Back to Springdale, refuel and repack

    Get out of the canyon before the midday heat. In July the shuttle stops are 100 °F by noon.

    Break Travel 40 min $18 per person
  • 1:00 PM Drive the Mount Carmel Highway

    Route 9 climbs the switchbacks and through the 1930 tunnel. Oversized vehicles need an escort and pay a fee, which is why the SUV size matters.

    Scenic drive · 1h 30m Travel 1h 45m · 72 mi $0
  • 4:00 PM Bryce Canyon, Sunset Point to Sunrise Point

    The rim trail between them is one flat mile with the best hoodoo views in the park. Bryce sits at 8,000 feet and is 20 °F cooler than Zion.

    Walk · 1h 30m Travel 25 min · 14 mi $0 with pass
  • 8:30 PM Stargazing at Bryce

    One of the darkest certified skies in the lower 48. Rangers run astronomy programs most summer evenings, free, first come.

    Activity · 1h Travel 10 min $0
Estimated daily cost $134 per person

Bryce lodging inside the park books eleven months ahead. Tropic, fifteen minutes east, is the practical fallback at about $95 per person.

03 Bryce → Capitol Reef → Moab Southwest loop · Day 3 of 3
  • 7:00 AM Sunrise at Bryce Point

    Fifteen minutes from the lodge and worth the alarm. Bring a jacket; it can be 38 °F at sunrise in June at 8,300 feet.

    Sightseeing · 1h Travel 12 min · 5 mi $0 with pass
  • 9:30 AM Highway 12 Scenic Byway

    One of the best drives in the country. The Hogback section has drop-offs on both sides and no shoulder. Take it slow and stop at the pullouts.

    Scenic drive · 2h 30m Travel 2h 30m · 112 mi $0
  • 12:30 PM Capitol Reef, Fruita and the orchards

    The least visited of Utah's five parks and the only one with a pie shop. Scenic Drive is eight miles and adds an hour.

    Park · 2h Travel 20 min · 9 mi $0 with pass
  • 4:00 PM Drive to Moab

    Two and a half hours through empty country. Fill the tank in Hanksville. The next reliable station is 68 miles away.

    Transit Travel 2h 30m · 146 mi $29 fuel per person
  • 7:30 PM Check in and dinner, Moab

    Moab is the base for Arches and Canyonlands. Rooms run $170 to $280 in spring and fall and drop hard in July when it hits 104 °F.

    Lodging Travel 10 min $108 per person

Getting around

You need a car and you should size it up. A mid-size SUV costs about $22 a day more than a compact and gives you room for a cooler, water, and the clearance for unpaved pullouts. It also matters at Zion, where oversized vehicles pay a tunnel escort fee.

Several parks run mandatory shuttles in season, including Zion Canyon, Bryce in summer, and Yosemite Valley. Shuttles are free, frequent, and they are how the parks manage parking that has not grown since 1965.

Download offline maps every single morning. Cell coverage inside the parks is close to zero, and that includes the parking lots. Google Maps offline areas and the free NPS app both work well.

Timed entry, in plain terms: a handful of parks now require a free or low-cost reservation for a specific arrival window during peak season. It is separate from your park pass, it releases on Recreation.gov on a published schedule, and popular slots go in under a minute. Entering before or after the window, usually before 7:00 AM or after 4:00 PM, skips the requirement entirely at most parks.

Where to stay

There are four options and they trade cost against how early you have to wake up. In-park lodging is the only one that lets you be at a trailhead at sunrise without a 5:00 AM drive.

In-park lodges

Zion Lodge, Bryce Canyon Lodge, Old Faithful Inn, Yosemite's Ahwahnee. Book 13 months out to the day. No televisions, spotty wifi, unbeatable location.

$210 to $460Book earliest

Park campgrounds

$20 to $40 a site. Reservable on Recreation.gov six months ahead at 10:00 AM Eastern. Some parks hold a portion as first come, first served.

$20 to $40Cheapest

Gateway towns

Springdale, Moab, Tropic, Estes Park, West Yellowstone. Walkable to shuttles at Zion, a short drive elsewhere. The default for most trips.

$150 to $310Most practical

Chain motels, 30 minutes out

Kanab, Green River, Hurricane, Gardiner. Half the price of the gateway town and an extra 25 to 40 minutes each way, twice a day.

$95 to $160Budget lever

Safety notes

Park rangers respond to the same handful of situations every summer. All of them are preventable and most of them start with water.

  • Heat and dehydration. One gallon per person per day in the desert, minimum, and more if you are hiking. Grand Canyon rangers rescue people every week who carried one bottle down and could not walk back up.
  • Flash floods. Slot canyons flood from storms you cannot see. The Narrows in Zion closes on flow rate, not on how the sky looks above you. Check the daily flash flood potential rating at the visitor center.
  • Altitude. Bryce rim is 8,300 feet, Rocky Mountain's Trail Ridge Road tops 12,183. Take the first day easy and drink more than you think you need.
  • Wildlife distance rules. 25 yards from bison, elk and deer. 100 yards from bears and wolves. Bison injure more Yellowstone visitors than bears do, by a wide margin.
  • Tell someone your plan. Leave the route and your expected return time with somebody who is not on the hike. Cell service does not exist where it matters.

Before you go

  • Emergency: 911, where there is coverage
  • Check nps.gov alerts for the specific park, that morning
  • Recreation.gov for permits and timed entry
  • Download the free NPS app and the offline map for each park
  • A paper map in the glovebox. Batteries die in the heat.

A satellite messenger is worth renting for any trip with backcountry days. About $40 a week.

Fees, permit systems and timed entry windows change annually. Confirm everything on nps.gov and recreation.gov before you travel. See our disclaimer.
